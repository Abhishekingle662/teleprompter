Shadows on the Wall
A Poem of Horror and Graffiti

In the dead of night, when fog rolls thick and low,
The city walls begin to breathe, to pulse, to glow.
Spray cans rattle like bones in cryptic hands,
As phantom artists mark forbidden lands.

Beneath the bridge where no one dares to tread,
Where concrete bleeds with colors of the dead,
The graffiti screams in twisted, violent hues—
Crimson reds and midnight blacks and sickly bruise.

Each tag a curse, each mural tells a tale,
Of souls who vanished on this urban trail.
The paint drips down like tears, like blood, like rain,
And whispers secrets of forgotten pain.

They say at 3 AM, the walls come alive,
The faces in the art begin to writhe and dive.
Eyes that follow you with hollow, empty stare,
Hands that reach from brick and mortar, clutching air.

A boy once sprayed his name in silver chrome,
But never made it back to safety, back to home.
His signature remains, still wet, still fresh, still bright,
Though decades passed since that accursed night.

The alley echoes with the hiss of aerosol screams,
Where reality dissolves and nothing's as it seems.
Each layer hides another darker layer still,
A palimpsest of terror, death, and ill will.

Beware the murals that appear at dawn,
By artists that the daylight swears are gone.
For those who dare to paint where shadows play,
Become the art themselves—and never fade away.

The wall remembers every hand that touched its face,
Every rebel soul who claimed this haunted space.
And when you see fresh paint on ancient stone,
Remember: some artists never work alone.

In tunnels deep where subway trains don't run,
Where graffiti glows without the help of sun,
The dead still tag, still throw, still leave their mark,
Forever painting nightmares in the dark.

So if you hear the rattle of a can at night,
And see strange symbols shimmering in streetlight,
Turn away, walk fast, don't look behind,
For some art galleries are better left unsigned.

The city is their canvas, vast and wide,
Where horror and creation coincide.
Where every wall might whisper, scream, or moan—
In this gallery of terror, carved in blood and bone.


---

END OF POEM

This piece explores the intersection of urban art and supernatural horror, 
where the act of creation becomes a haunting ritual and the walls themselves 
become living witnesses to dark mysteries.
